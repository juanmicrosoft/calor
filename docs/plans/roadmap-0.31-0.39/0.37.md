## Epic #1698 · [0.37] Epic: earn the language

Existing epic #1698. The body below replaces or amends it as marked.

#### Outcome
Determine where maintained Calor source adds useful value over equally capable C# workflows, including adoption and maintenance costs. This is a falsifiable language/workflow hypothesis, not a promised positive result.

It is a confirmation, under fairer controls, of one probe-scale positive. It is not a first test. The record it starts from:

- **One hit, at fixture scale.** In `ws5-probe-001` (2026-07-28, pre-0.30 syntax, nine defects seeded by the project in its own fixtures, three runs per condition) the Calor condition caught 9 of 9 defects and conventional C# caught 4 of 9: a difference of 5 against a threshold of 3 fixed in advance (`bench/phase0-agent-native/epochs/ws5-probe-001/VERDICT.md:21-26`; adjudicated a hit at `bench/phase0-agent-native/epochs/m5-compare-001/VERDICT.md:83-85`). For the three contract defects the C# condition carried the contract only as a doc comment (`ws5-probe-001/VERDICT.md:64-68`). Calor took 1.63 iterations against 1.0 and 247,304 output tokens against 69,545 (`ws5-probe-001/VERDICT.md:72-73`).
- **One miss, at real scale.** The real-scale venue was adjudicated a miss and retired on 2026-08-05 (`docs/plans/call-s-adjudication.md:3-5`).
- **One replication.** The 0.32 run (#1810) repeats the nine defects on the syntax as it stands after the 0.32 breaking window, with a third condition, C# with executable specifications. Its outcome is recorded under Entry criteria.

What this epic adds to that record: semantically equipped C# as the comparator, tasks and acceptance checks owned by someone other than the implementing agents, blinded defect controls, results per task stratum fixed in advance, and full engineering cost.

#### Entry criteria
Preparation (#1708 to #1711, #1865, #1857) may start earlier. #1712 starts only when all three are recorded, each with a link, in a comment on this epic.

1. **Adopter branch.** Either 0.36 pilot acceptance, meaning #1697 is closed on its exit record (posted as a comment on that epic and entered in the evidence ledger), or the adopter checkpoint (#1833) recorded "demand unproven" and this epic runs at the fallback scope below.
2. **Obligation boundary.** #1702 has landed, or the comment states that no task is counted as effect-boundary evidence (evidence that an effect row or a contract stopped a wrong change). "Landed" means its pilot-independent half is on `main`: the policy, the checker, and the command that classifies a base-to-head change as implementation-only or obligation-changing, with their demonstrations. Activation on a pilot repository is not required.
3. **Replication recorded.** The outcome of #1810 (the Calor-minus-equipped-C# delta out of nine defects, "not adjudicated", or "declined"), and the paragraph "What changes in the #1698 hypothesis" that the #1816 registration wrote in advance for that outcome, quoted from the registration. It registers four outcomes. A delta of at least 3 of 9 is a language effect at fixture scale: this epic tests whether the effect holds on independently authored tasks. A smaller delta means the July result was mostly "specifications were present": the hypothesis narrows as the registration states, and the #1708 targets and the sizing (#1786) are refreshed for the smaller expected difference before #1867 is decided. Not adjudicated: record "replication not adjudicated" with the reason. Declined: record "no equipped-C# reading exists at fixture scale".

#### If the adopter checkpoint is not passed
The fallback is written in advance, as a table, in the plan of record, `docs/plans/roadmap-0.31-0.39.md` (#1765). The table gives the disposition of each of #1708 to #1712 and of #1865 to #1867 (re-scoped or closed); says what a fallback 0.37 adds beyond the 0.32 replication, or that this epic closes on the existing results with no new run; names the inventory that bounds any proof claim when there is no pilot component; and defines the cases "verdict not collected" and "verdict not positive". This epic cites that table and does not restate it. Four rules of this epic hold whatever the table keeps: paid collection still needs #1867; results from tasks whose requirements or acceptance checks were written by the maintainer or the implementing agents are labelled non-independent (#1708); 0.38 and 0.39 stay held; and nothing published at fallback scope opens 0.38.

#### Work breakdown
Child issues cover independent, stratified tasks and economics (#1708); three comparable authoring conditions (#1709); reuse of the multi-agent harness without search (#1710); blinded implementation, specification and boundary-defect controls, which are the primary source of discrimination between conditions (#1711); three inputs the study stop of 2026-09-09 recorded as absent (`docs/plans/safe-delegation-m0/decision.md:18-21`, `:43-52`), namely a count of task supply (#1865), an independent methods review (#1857, by the reviewer named under #1777) and an authorised budget (#1867); and approved execution with a published investment decision (#1712), which also commits the executable analysis, with its rules for undefined ratios, before collection starts.

#### Comparison
1. Conventional C# tooling.
2. C# with comparable explicit specifications and validation capabilities wherever practical ("semantically equipped C#": analyzers, executable assertions and disposal analyzers, bolted on).
3. Calor with integrated contracts, effects, and applicable verification.

Condition 2 is the single gating comparator: the verdict is decided on condition 3 against condition 2. Condition 1 is reported; it separates what executable specifications alone buy (2 against 1) from what the language adds (3 against 2). A Rust condition may be reported on eligible tasks (#1709). It is never adjudicated and no verdict statement depends on it.

All receive the same behavioral requirements and independent acceptance criteria. Disclose unavoidable capability differences and charge setup/maintenance costs fairly. Do not handicap C# or constrain its algorithms to Calor's representation.

#### Acceptance
- Use 0.36 pilot findings and a developer-valued, currently supportable performance task plus a harder task exposing real limits.
- Tasks are assigned to strata fixed in advance (effect-and-contract, neutral, C#-favoured), and every result is reported per stratum (#1708). Tasks are not chosen because Calor is expected to win them.
- Pin model/tool/source/build configurations, control budgets and order effects, record assistance and invalid runs, and justify repetitions from variability and available budget.
- Count specification authoring, integration, agent/validation execution, review, rework, and maintenance. Scope/risk is independently defined, not lines changed.
- Separate seeded-defect detection from natural failure rates; zero observed defects is not equivalent safety.
- No task is counted as effect-boundary evidence unless #1702 has landed and the task meets the rule in #1711. Contracts derived mechanically from the implementation are labelled "derived" and never counted as specification-backed proofs (#1709, #1711).
- Publish reproducible wins, losses, uncertainty, costs, and limitations, particularly against semantically equipped C#.
- Exit. The milestone exits on one of three records, linked from a comment on this epic:
  1. **Verdict published.** #1712 is closed, with its proceed/narrow/revise/stop decision published in the 0.37 release notes and entered in the evidence ledger (#1774).

  3. **Fallback disposition recorded.** Under the adopter fallback, a comment on this epic links the #1833 record and the table and states the option. Under F1 (no new run) that comment is the exit record: the verdict is recorded as "not collected: no independent tasks" and this epic closes when #1833 is applied.
